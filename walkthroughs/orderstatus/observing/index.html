Observing

What the running order status processor tells you in Grafana on kind: a record’s trace across the topics in Tempo, its log records in Loki, the processor’s, humus’s, the group’s lag and the containers’ restarts in Mimir, the alert rules in deploy/alerts.yaml, and the dashboard in deploy/dashboard.json.

Opening Grafana

up.sh generates Grafana’s admin password into the grafana-admin Secret. Read it, then forward Grafana’s port:

kubectl --context kind-kind --namespace orderstatus get secret grafana-admin \
  --output jsonpath='{.data.password}' | base64 --decode
kubectl --context kind-kind --namespace orderstatus port-forward service/grafana 3000:3000

Sign in at http://localhost:3000 as admin.

A record’s trace

Produce an order’s events with orderevents, as Deploying shows. In Explore, choose Tempo and find the latest records’ traces:

{resource.service.name="orderstatus" && name="process orders.events"}
SpanWhat it is
process orders.eventsthe record’s processing, with its partition and offset
send orders.statuseach change of status it produced
send orders.retrythe record, produced for another attempt
process orders.retrythat attempt, a child of the send orders.retry that produced it
send orders.dlqthe dead letter

So every attempt at a record is in one trace. A test of the App checks it, for a record retried once, then dead-lettered:

refused := processSpan(t, spans, eventsTopic, 1)
require.Equal(t, []string{"send " + retryTopic}, childNames(spans, refused))
retry := childNamed(t, spans, refused, "send "+retryTopic)
retried := processSpan(t, spans, retryTopic, 0)
require.Equal(t, retry.SpanContext().SpanID(), retried.Parent().SpanID())
require.Equal(t, refused.SpanContext().TraceID(), retried.SpanContext().TraceID())
require.Equal(t, []string{"send " + deadLetterTopic}, childNames(spans, retried))

A record’s key, the order’s ID, is on no span, so search by the topic, the partition and the offset:

{resource.service.name="orderstatus" && span.messaging.destination.partition.id="3" && span.messaging.kafka.offset=42}

Its log records

In Explore, with Loki, the processor’s records are:

{service_name="orderstatus"}
RecordWritten by
serving, shutting downthe App, as it starts and stops
partitions assigned, partitions revoked, partitions lostthe Service, on each rebalance, with the topic and the partitions
circuit breaker open, circuit breaker half-open, circuit breaker closedeach Producer’s breaker, as it changes state
franz-go’s own, at INFO and abovethe Service’s and the Producers’ clients
the cause, at ERRORthe App, when it stops on a record, which it names by topic, partition and offset

The records of a crashed processor are its errors:

{service_name="orderstatus"} | severity_text="ERROR"

A record with a trace ID links to its trace in Tempo:

    jsonData:
      derivedFields:
        - name: trace_id
          matcherType: label
          matcherRegex: trace_id
          datasourceUid: tempo
          url: "$${__value.raw}"
          urlDisplayLabel: Trace

Its metrics

The processor counts the records it retried and dead-lettered, by orderstatus.reason, as What it counts shows:

p.dropped.Add(ctx, 1, metric.WithAttributes(reasonKey.String(reason)))

In Mimir, a name’s dots are underscores, and service.name is job:

sum by (orderstatus_reason) (rate(orderstatus_dead_letters{job="orderstatus"}[5m]))

humus records its own:

InstrumentRecorded by
messaging.client.consumed.messages, messaging.process.durationthe Service, for each record, by topic and partition
messaging.client.operation.durationthe Service, for each commit, and the Producers, for each record
messaging.client.sent.messagesthe Producers, by topic
humus.circuit.state, humus.circuit.calls, humus.circuit.transitionseach Producer’s breaker, scope github.com/z5labs/humus/queue/kafka
messaging.kafka.*franz-go’s clients, scope github.com/twmb/franz-go/plugin/kotel

The Collector reads the group’s lag from Kafka, and the containers' restarts from Kubernetes. A processor that has crashed reports neither:

  kafkametrics:
    brokers: [kafka:9093]
    tls:
      ca_file: /etc/otel-collector-kafka/ca.crt
      cert_file: /etc/otel-collector-kafka/tls.crt
      key_file: /etc/otel-collector-kafka/tls.key
    scrapers: [consumers]
    group_match: ^orderstatus$
    topic_match: ^orders\.(events|retry)$
    collection_interval: 10s
  k8s_cluster:
    auth_type: serviceAccount
    collection_interval: 10s

Its login, CN=otel-collector, may describe the group and the two topics it reads, and nothing else:

acl otel-collector --operation Describe --group orderstatus
acl otel-collector --operation Describe --operation DescribeConfigs --topic orders.events --topic orders.retry

kafka_consumer_group_lag{group="orderstatus", topic="orders.events"}
k8s_container_restarts{k8s_container_name="orderstatus"}

The alerts

deploy/alerts.yaml holds Grafana-managed alert rules, in the folder Order status. The kind overlay provisions them:

  - name: grafana-alerting
    files:
      - ../alerts.yaml
AlertSeverityWhen
OrderStatusLagHighpagethe group has been more than 1000 records behind on orders.events for 10 minutes
OrderStatusStalledpagea partition of orders.events has records waiting, and no commit in 5 minutes
OrderStatusCrashLoopingpagean orderstatus container restarted more than 3 times in 15 minutes
OrderStatusDeadLettersticketa record was dead-lettered in the last 15 minutes
OrderStatusRetryingticketrecords have been sent to orders.retry for 10 minutes
OrderStatusBreakerOpenpagea Producer’s breaker is open
OrderStatusSlowProcessingpagethe p99 of processing a record of orders.events has been over 5s for 10 minutes

The lag rules read orders.events only, since a record in orders.retry waits there on purpose.

Each rule is a query of Mimir and a threshold:

      - uid: order-status-lag-high
        title: OrderStatusLagHigh
        condition: B
        data:
          - refId: A
            relativeTimeRange:
              from: 600
              to: 0
            datasourceUid: mimir
            model:
              refId: A
              instant: true
              expr: sum(clamp_min(kafka_consumer_group_lag{group="orderstatus", topic="orders.events"}, 0)) or vector(0)
          - refId: B
            datasourceUid: __expr__
            model:
              refId: B
              type: threshold
              expression: A
              conditions:
                - evaluator:
                    type: gt
                    params: [1000]
        noDataState: OK
        execErrState: Error
        for: 10m
        labels:
          severity: page

A counter’s rule adds the series that first appeared in its window, which increase alone reads as no change:

              expr: sum by (orderstatus_reason) ((increase(orderstatus_dead_letters{job="orderstatus"}[15m]) > 0) or (orderstatus_dead_letters{job="orderstatus"} unless orderstatus_dead_letters{job="orderstatus"} offset 15m)) or on () vector(0)

The dashboard

deploy/dashboard.json is the Order status dashboard, at http://localhost:3000/d/orderstatus. The kind overlay provisions it from a file, so it can’t be changed in Grafana:

apiVersion: 1
providers:
  - name: orderstatus
    orgId: 1
    folder: Order status
    type: file
    disableDeletion: true
    allowUiUpdates: false
    options:
      path: /etc/grafana/dashboards
PanelWhat it shows
Lag of the group orderstatusthe lag on orders.events and orders.retry
Records processedthe records consumed, by topic
Records retried by reasonorderstatus.retries, as a rate
Records dead-lettered by reasonorderstatus.dead_letters, as a rate
Processing time of orders.eventsits mean and p99
Producers’ breakersthe Producers’ breakers, by state
Restartseach orderstatus container’s restarts

Each panel is a query of Mimir:

      "targets": [
        {
          "refId": "A",
          "datasource": {
            "type": "prometheus",
            "uid": "mimir"
          },
          "expr": "sum by (orderstatus_reason) (rate(orderstatus_dead_letters{job=\"orderstatus\"}[$__rate_interval]))",
          "legendFormat": "{{orderstatus_reason}}"
        }
      ]
